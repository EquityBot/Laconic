# Australian Government Style Manual: editing rules

This required reference keeps the mechanics used in ordinary Australian English prose. The complete rules and examples from the seventh edition, scraped 13 September 2026, are preserved in [style-manual-detail.md](style-manual-detail.md), under the same headings. Source: [Australian Government Style Manual](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/), Commonwealth of Australia, CC BY 4.0.

## When to apply it

Apply the mechanics below to all Australian English prose. Read and apply [style-manual-detail.md](style-manual-detail.md) in full for Australian Government, public sector or Australian corporate content, requested Style Manual compliance or plain language. This includes its register, structure, inclusive language and accessibility rules.

For other prose, preserve the writer's register. Read the relevant detailed section whenever a rule, exception, grammatical role or wording choice needs clarification, or the task specifically concerns that topic. The user's requirements, factual fidelity, reader comprehension and a human writer's voice take priority over stylistic preference.

For legislation, cases, ATO rulings and explanatory memoranda, read [aglc4-citation.md](aglc4-citation.md). It governs inside citations. For an uncovered rule, search [australian-government-style-manual.md](australian-government-style-manual.md) for the topic; never read that 1.3 MB corpus end to end. ## Australian English mechanics

### Spelling

Use the first spelling in the Macquarie Dictionary. Record deliberate departures. Prefer acknowledgement, lodgement and judgement; legal material uses judgment. Keep explicit user choices and protected quotations, names, code, paths, URLs and identifiers exact.

Choose forms by meaning and grammatical role: licence is a noun, license a verb; practice is a noun, practise a verb; dependant is a person, dependent an adjective. Prefer focused, focusing and adviser; focussed and focussing remain accepted variants. Preserve Advisor in official titles. Write wellbeing. Inspect grammatical pairs individually; do not use global replacement. Read the detailed Spelling section for its complete confused-pair list and fixed forms when proofreading them. Use that for essential clauses and which with a comma pair for non-essential clauses.

### Capitals

Use sentence case and minimise capitals. Capitalise proper names, including Australian Government; lower case generic references such as the government, the department and the program. Capitalise an official title before its holder's name; lower case a generic role. Expanded acronyms follow ordinary capitalisation.

### Percentages, quotes and titles

Write per cent, never percent, in prose; percentage is the noun. Use numerals with a symbol with no space before it: `12%`, `0.4%`. Single quotation marks are the default; double marks enclose a quotation within a quotation. Italicise Act and case titles in running prose; use roman type in reference lists and for bills.

### Punctuation

Use only punctuation needed for grammar and meaning, with one space after a sentence. No full stops after headings, headers, footers, captions or between acronym letters, or after most abbreviations and contractions. Put quotation punctuation inside when it belongs to the quotation, outside when it belongs to the sentence. Read the detailed section for attribution and competing punctuation.

### Dashes

The house rule prohibits em dashes in original prose. Preserve protected quotations and literals. Government digital content uses spaced en dashes, sparingly, for non-essential content that can be removed without breaking the sentence. Do not mix dash styles for one purpose.

Prefer words for spans: from 57 to 65, between 1970 and 1980. Never combine from or between with a dash. Preserve unspaced en dashes in calendar spans, titles, terms of office and lifespans, or where space is limited; use 4 digits on each side for those exceptions for date spans. Financial years use the house hyphen: `2026-27`. Inspect and fix them individually, never by global rewriting. Coordinate nouns use an en dash, such as Murray–Darling; a hyphen can change the meaning. Keep minus, hyphen and dash characters distinct. Read the detailed Dashes section for technical codes and quoted interruptions.

### Hyphens

Remove a hyphen when the phrase reads the same without it. Keep set phrases open: open source, plain language, public sector. Close established compounds: email, online, website, codebase, dataset, timeframe, roadmap, coordinate. Resolve uncertain compounds from a dictionary or the scraped manual.

Temporary compound modifiers take a hyphen before a noun only when needed, and stay open after it: up-to-date accounts; accounts are up to date. Compounds with 2 adjectives or a noun plus an adjective keep it in both positions. Never hyphenate compounds modified by very, particularly, least or most. Preserve meaningful participle, numeral and fixed compounds: well-known, 4-part, forty-five, go-ahead, mother-in-law. An adverb ending in -ly takes no hyphen; fully-fashioned and fully-fledged are exceptions.

Keep doubled-vowel prefixes, meaningful distinctions and established non-, self-, sub- and anti- forms: re-enter, re-sign versus resign. Query uncertain meaning. Preserve every hyphen in protected literals. Write financial years with a hyphen. Avoid hanging hyphens by repeating the word. Replace empty hyphenated buzzwords with supported facts; preserve precise terms and proper names. Read the full Hyphens section for exceptions and dictionary forms.

### Numbers

In general prose, write zero and one as words and 2 upwards as numerals. Use words at sentence starts, for general fractions, proper names and figures of speech, and numbers below 10 in journalistic content. Use numerals for units, time, comparisons, related series, tables, charts, dates, steps, ages, school years and scientific content. Technical formats take priority.

Group thousands with commas, never spaces. Combine large rounded values with words: 1.4 million, $50 billion. Either one million or 1 million is valid; prefer the numeral in technical, number-dense, related series and currency contexts. Money and percentages take numerals and symbols, except general money approximations. Write first to ninth, then 10th upwards; centuries take numerals. Compare like with like and preserve bounds and units.

### Dates and time

Use day month year with no commas: 22 July 2024. Use a non-breaking space between day and month. Numeric dates are d/m/yyyy where space is tight; 2-digit years are limited to financial data or unmistakable contexts. Times use a colon, a space and lower case am or pm; use a non-breaking space and consistent trailing zeros. Avoid duplicated morning/am or afternoon/pm. Write noon, midday or midnight. Decades take no apostrophe: 1980s.

### Shortened forms

Define unfamiliar acronyms on first use; reverse the definition for a widely known form such as DNA. NT, ACT, am, pm, Qantas, BHP, radar and scuba need no definition. Keep terms spelt out when used only once or twice. A full agency name followed by its generic noun can be clearer than an acronym. Repeat definitions where readers may enter long content midway; add a glossary for numerous specialist terms.

No full stops in acronyms, initialisms or contractions, except sentence punctuation. Avoid plurals or possessives at first definition. Prefer for example and that is to Latin abbreviations. If e.g. or i.e. is necessary, retain both full stops and add no comma. Contractions suit standard and informal prose; avoid them in formal content. Read the full section for exceptions.
