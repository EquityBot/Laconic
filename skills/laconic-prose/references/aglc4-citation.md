# AGLC4: citing Australian law, for tax and accounting work

The Australian Guide to Legal Citation, 4th edition (Melbourne University Law Review and Melbourne Journal of International Law, 2018) is the standard Australian legal citation system. This page is an original summary of the rules that come up in tax and accounting content, not a copy of the guide. AGLC4 is copyright and published free as a view-only PDF, so it is not reproduced here.

The free PDF (336 pages) is available from law.unimelb.edu.au. It omits the appendices, so the full abbreviation tables for report series and pinpoints are not in it. For anything this page does not cover, check that PDF rather than guessing.

## When AGLC applies, and what beats it

Use AGLC inside a legal citation: statute names, section pinpoints, case citations, rulings, explanatory memoranda. Use the Australian Government Style Manual for the prose around the citation. Where they disagree inside the citation, AGLC wins; where they disagree in the sentence, the Style Manual wins. See [style-manual-rules.md](style-manual-rules.md).

The disagreements that actually bite:

| Point | AGLC inside a citation | Style Manual in prose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spans | Unspaced en dash: `ss 42–9`, `[50]–[59]` | Words: 'from 2017 to 2019', en dash only for financial years and the other listed exceptions |
| Shortened forms | `ibid`, `(n 46)`, `cf`, `eg` in citations | Prefer English: 'for example', 'that is' |
| Number spans | Shortened: `121–7`, `312–13` | Its own number rules apply to body text |
| Full stops in abbreviations | None: `ss`, `reg`, `No`, `Cth` | Also none |

## Acts

Format: *Title Year* (Jurisdiction) pinpoint. Title and year in italics, jurisdiction upright in parentheses.

- *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* (Cth) s 8-1
- *Income Tax Assessment Act 1936* (Cth) pt III div 7A
- *A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999* (Cth) s 9-5
- *Taxation Administration Act 1953* (Cth) sch 1 s 12-35
- *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth) s 9 (definition of 'financial product')

Rules that catch people out:

- The year is the year of original enactment, never the year of the amendment or the compilation you are reading. An `Income Tax Assessment Act 1997` citation stays 1997 no matter which compilation applies.
- Cite the principal Act, not the amending Act, unless you are deliberately showing legislative history.
- Jurisdiction abbreviations: Cth, ACT, NSW, NT, Qld, SA, Tas, Vic, WA.
- Where Parliament numbered 2 Acts with the same name, keep the number: *Financial Framework Legislation Amendment Act (No 2) 2012* (Cth).
- Bills follow the same shape but take no italics, and usually take `cl` rather than `s`.
- The Constitution is cited as *Australian Constitution* s 51(ii), with no year and no jurisdiction.

## Pinpoints

The abbreviation goes before the number with a space; the subsection goes in parentheses with no space.

| Designation | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section | s | ss |
| Subsection | sub-s | sub-ss |
| Paragraph | para | paras |
| Part | pt | pts |
| Division | div | divs |
| Subdivision | sub-div | sub-divs |
| Schedule | sch | schs |
| Clause | cl | cls |
| Chapter | ch | chs |
| Regulation | reg | regs |
| Rule | r | rr |
| Order | ord | ords |

- `s 8-1(1)(a)`, never `s 8-1 (1)(a)` and never `sub-s 8-1(1)`. Use the abbreviation for the highest level named in the pinpoint: a section with a subsection is still `s`.
- Stack levels with spaces and no commas: `pt III div 2`, `ch 2 pt 1 div 4`.
- **Tax statutes use a hyphen, not an en dash.** `s 8-1`, `s 20-110(1)(a)`, `s 995-1`. The hyphen is part of the section number. Getting this wrong is the single most common error in Australian tax citations.
- Decimal numbering takes the lowest level cited: `s 3.2.1`, not `ch 3.2.1`.
- Section numbering runs through an Act, so do not name the part or division when citing a section.
- Definitions: *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* (Cth) s 995-1 (definition of 'trading stock'). Where the definition has lettered paragraphs, add `para (a)` inside the parentheses with no comma before it.

## Multiple pinpoints

- Plural abbreviation once, at the front: `ss 5, 7, 9, 28`.
- Consecutive pinpoints take an unspaced en dash: `ss 42–9`, `sub-ss (2)–(7)`, `divs 2–3`.
- Non-consecutive pinpoints take commas, never 'and': `ss 92(1), (4), (7)`.
- Alphanumeric and decimal sections are written out in full on both sides: `ss 2A–2D`, not `ss 2A–D`; `ss 3.2.1–3.2.2`, not `ss 3.2.1–2`.
- Paragraph spans keep both sets of brackets: `[56]–[59]`, not `[56–9]`.

## Delegated legislation

Same shape as an Act, with `reg`, `r`, `ord` pinpoints.

- *Income Tax Assessment (1997 Act) Regulations 2021* (Cth) reg 307-205.01
- *Migration Regulations 1994* (Cth) regs 2.01–2.02

## ATO rulings, determinations and other instruments

Rulings are not delegated legislation and are not gazetted, so they take the instrumentality form:

Instrumentality, *Instrument Title* (Document Number, Full Date) pinpoint.

- Australian Taxation Office, *Income Tax: Division 7A: Unpaid Present Entitlements* (TR 2010/3, 2 June 2010)
- Australian Securities and Investments Commission, *ASIC Corporations Instrument* (Instrument Number, Full Date) [4]

Points to get right:

- The title is italicised; the document number and date sit in parentheses, unitalicised.
- Include the document number only when it appears on the instrument, and reproduce its abbreviations exactly with no full stops.
- The date is the date the instrument takes effect, not the date you downloaded it.
- Name the office, never the person holding it. Add the jurisdiction in parentheses after a department or officer: Minister for Immigration and Border Protection (Cth).
- A body such as the ASX or a professional association drops Pty, Ltd, Inc and a leading 'the', and where there is no document number takes `(at Full Date)` instead.

## Explanatory memoranda

Explanatory Memorandum, Bill Citation pinpoint.

- Explanatory Memorandum, Treasury Laws Amendment Bill 2024 (Cth) 12 [1.24]

Use 'Explanatory Statement' or 'Explanatory Notes' where that jurisdiction does. The Bill citation follows the Bill rules, so no italics. Second reading speeches are parliamentary debates, cited under a different rule.

## Legislative history

Link the 2 Acts with a comma and the exact expression; the terms are not interchangeable.

- *Income Tax Assessment Act 1936* (Cth) s 109D, as amended by *Treasury Laws Amendment Act 2020* (Cth) sch 1 item 4
- *Income Tax Assessment Act 1936* (Cth) s 109D, as at 30 June 2024
- *Crimes Act 1914* (Cth) s 24A(g), as repealed by *Anti-Terrorism Act (No 2) 2005* (Cth) sch 7 item 2

Available expressions: as enacted, as at *date*, as amended by, later amended by, amending, as repealed by, repealing, as inserted by, inserting.

## Cases

Format: *Case Name* (Year) Volume Report Series StartPage, pinpoint (Judge).

- *Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Day* (2008) 236 CLR 163, 175 [28] (Gummow, Hayne, Heydon and Kiefel JJ)

Rules:

- Round brackets when the report series numbers its volumes; square brackets when it organises by year: *King v King* [1974] Qd R 253.
- Prefer the authorised report. The order of preference runs authorised report, then generalist unauthorised report, then subject-specific unauthorised report, then unreported with a medium neutral citation, then unreported without one.
- Authorised series for the courts that matter here: CLR for the High Court, FCR for the Federal Court, then the state series (NSWLR, VR, Qd R, SASR, WAR, Tas R, NTLR, ACTLR). Common unauthorised series: ALR, ALJR, FLR, ATC.
- **No parallel citations.** Cite one version only, not `(1999) 198 CLR 180; 164 ALR 606; [1999] HCA 36`.
- Pinpoint to the page, with the paragraph optionally added: `163, 175 [28]`. Include the page even when the pinpoint is the first page.
- Unreported with a medium neutral citation: *Case Name* [Year] Court Identifier Judgment Number, [pinpoint], for example `[2009] TASCC 80, [11]`.
- Name the court only when it is not otherwise obvious, in parentheses after the pinpoint.
- Report series names are never italicised; case names always are.

## Subsequent references

- **First citation is always in full**, in the text and in the first footnote.
- Introduce a short title in single inverted commas inside parentheses at the end of that first citation: *Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977* (Cth) ('*ADJR Act*'). The short title keeps whatever italics the full title had.
- After that, cite the short title with a cross-reference to the footnote holding the full citation: *ADJR Act* (n 46) s 7. Not a bare *ADJR Act* s 7.
- Secondary sources use the author's surname: Rubenstein (n 59) 48.
- `Ibid` refers only to the immediately preceding footnote, and only when that footnote holds a single source. Capitalise it at the start of a footnote. Repeat the pinpoint only when it differs, with no comma: `Ibid 404 [32]`.
- Where the same source appears twice in one footnote, use `at` before the later pinpoint rather than repeating the citation.
- For legislation, the title always appears in the footnote even when it appears in the sentence above. For cases, the name may be dropped from the footnote when it appears in full in the accompanying sentence, but never in a subsequent reference.
- For a portion of an Act, put the short title after the pinpoint: *Competition and Consumer Act 2010* (Cth) sch 2 ('*Australian Consumer Law*'). Later pinpoints then run inside that portion.

## Parallel Acts across jurisdictions

Commonwealth first, then states and territories alphabetically by jurisdiction abbreviation, separated by semicolons.
